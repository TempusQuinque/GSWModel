Eastern Front Model 1941–42

The real campaign is the baseline. Each change the scenario makes acts through a stated mechanism whose strength is uncertain, so every result is a range from 1,500 runs: the middle figure, with the 10th to 90th percentile below it. With every assumption set to reality, the model returns the real figures.

How the model works
  • Baseline. Krivosheev's 1941 operations by phase (frontier, summer, autumn) and sector (Army Groups North, Centre, South), with their losses, durations and depths; Halder's German casualties; Bekker's Luftwaffe losses; Soviet tank stocks of 22,600 (June 1941) and 7,700 (January 1942).
  • Pace. Each phase's speed is the German-to-Soviet combat power ratio, relative to reality, raised to an uncertain power, times a readiness factor while the Soviets are alerted (slower frontier battles). Autumn progress slows in the mud (10 October–15 November) and again in the frost, and stops at the supply cap.
  • Pockets. The men in a pocket scale with Soviet strength; the share captured scales with German sealing power (infantry and the Luftwaffe) against the Soviet men, and with how fast the pincers close; a leak factor divides it in the frontier and summer battles while the Soviets are alerted. Men are conserved: escapers partly become casualties, and extra prisoners are not also counted as killed.
  • Fighting losses. Soviet killed and wounded scale with Soviet strength, German combat power and the length of each phase. German casualties follow the Soviet killed and wounded and the German men engaged, lowered by air support and raised by the alert's fighting effect.
  • Equipment. Soviet tanks lost follow the tanks engaged; guns follow pockets and fighting; aircraft follow Luftwaffe strength, with fewer destroyed on the ground when alerted. German tanks and aircraft follow Soviet opposition and the Luftwaffe's own size.
  • Winter. Soviet strike power and German defence are each a weighted product of men, tanks and aircraft relative to reality. Soviet losses scale with how hard they attack; German losses with the pressure and the German men in the line. Sickness and frost scale with men and time.
  • Spring. Stocks carry forward with production (German output for the army raised; Soviet output lowered without Lend-Lease) and replacements. The Moscow offensive's pace is a Typhoon-like 20–30 km a day scaled by the force ratio and cut by the prepared defences.

Two results are assumptions rather than findings: the ground taken in 1941, set by the supply limit, and the article's pace of the Moscow offensive, set chiefly by the assumed strength of the Moscow defences. An independent review of the first version found errors in the loss formulas, the lever wiring and the winter balance; they are corrected in this version, and the real run is checked against the baseline for any draw of the uncertain parameters. The ranges cover uncertainty in the strength of each mechanism, not in the model's structure. Figures marked approximate in the source file (some autumn equipment losses, the Soviet field strengths, the spring 1942 stocks) are estimates. The model is a check on the scenario's internal consistency, not a forecast.
